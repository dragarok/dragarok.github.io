Each Veda is compiled into two sections: Samhitā and Brāhmana. Sanātana Dharma considers Samhitā to be a revelation of supreme knowledge, while Brāhmana is commentary on the Samhitā by self-realized seers.

The Brāhmana section is further divided into two commentaries: Brāhmana and Āranyaka. Brāhmana provides instructions about the use of Vedic mantras in ritual, and the methods of sacrificial ceremony. Āranyaka is an exposition for study, analysis, and reflection upon Vedic mantras. In other words, the method of applying Vedic mantras in religious ceremonies is the subject of Brāhmana, and the understanding of the spiritual principles underlying the use of mantra is the subject of Āranyaka.

Vedic literature concludes with the teaching of the Upanishads. The Upanishads are the gist of the knowledge contained in the Samhitā, Brāhmana, and Āranyaka.
