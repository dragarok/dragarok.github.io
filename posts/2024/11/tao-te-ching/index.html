Tao Te Ching

Lao Tzu (tr. Stephen Mitchell)

Theme #1 Teachings on non-action

Less and less do you need to force things, until finally you arrive at non-action. When nothing is done, nothing is left undone

There is nothing to be done because it all accumulates to nothing in the very end. If you start to do something, there will always be something more to do. If nothing is done, then there is nothing left to be done as well. This is metaphorical where you need to understand that you wouldn’t have anything to do in this state; not just choosing to not do anything though you want to do it. This is non-action.

Personal

I was an astute believer of law of attraction but now I don’t like to force things. I believe there is even bigger force than law of attraction where you attract what you need without thinking about whether there could be a better choice to do.

A good athlete can enter a state of body-awareness in which the right stroke or the right movement happens by itself, effortlessly, without any interference of the conscious will. This is a paradigm for non-action: the purest and most effective form of action. The game plays the game; the poem writes the poem; we can’t tell the dancer from the dance.

Personal Experience

This is basically referring to the flow state where you act but aren’t aware of the act. Though it’s you that acts, it always feels like agency of something more than you; something outside of you.

Do your work, then step back. The only path to serenity.

If you attach yourself to the work you did, you will always feel like you have more to do. More to accomplish. More ego on what you already did.

Because he has no goal in mind, everything he does succeeds

What do you say to a man who sees everything happening the way it should happen given that it happens? The complete obeisance to the nature of any event that happens, it automatically comes as a success because that person believes in a bigger picture that’s at play making those things happen.

The Master does nothing, yet he leaves nothing undone. The ordinary man is always doing things, yet many more are left to be done.

Doing is the idea of an ordinary man. So, doing is always associated to your capability to perform. As long as you feel you’re able, you’ll find something more to do: a never ending cycle.

In the pursuit of knowledge, every day something is added. In the practice of the Tao, every day something is dropped. Less and less do you need to force things, until finally you arrive at non-action. When nothing is done, nothing is left undone.

Deeply profound.

He doesn’t think about his actions; they flow from the core of his being. He holds nothing back from life; therefore he is ready for death, as a man is ready for sleep after a good day’s work.

Rushing into action, you fail. Trying to grasp things, you lose them. Forcing a project to completion, you ruin what was almost ripe

Theme #2 Teacher

What is a good man but a bad man’s teacher? What is a bad man but a good man’s job?

The Master acts without doing anything and teaches without saying anything. Things arise and she lets them come; things disappear and she lets them go. She has but doesn’t possess, acts but doesn’t expect. When her work is done, she forgets it. That is why it lasts forever.

He helps people lose everything they know, everything they desire, and creates confusion in those who think that they know.

When the Master governs, the people are hardly aware that he exists. Next best is a leader who is loved. Next, one who is feared. The worst is one who is despised.

The Master doesn’t talk, he acts. When his work is done, the people say, “Amazing: we did it, all by ourselves!”

Thus the Master travels all day without leaving home. However splendid the views, she stays serenely in herself.

The world is formed from the void, like utensils from a block of wood. The Master knows the utensils, yet keeps to the block: thus she can use all things.

The Master views the parts with compassion, because he understands the whole. His constant practice is humility.

Quote 51 The Master

Thus the Master is content to serve as an example and not to impose her will. She is pointed, but doesn’t pierce. Straightforward, but supple. Radiant, but easy on the eyes.

Therefore the Master steps back so that people won’t be confused. He teaches without a teaching, so that people will have nothing to learn

By teaching without a teaching, people have nothing to cling to as an idea. They have to explore the knowledge themselves, and that process is the least confusing.

Theme #3 Teaching on topics except inaction

When people see some things as beautiful, other things become ugly.

When you are content to be simply yourself and don’t compare or compete, everybody will respect you.

Fill your bowl to the brim and it will spill. Keep sharpening your knife and it will blunt.

When there is no peace in the family,filial piety begins. When the country falls into chaos, patriotism is born.

Throw away holiness and wisdom, and people will be a hundred times happier. Throw away morality and justice, and people will do the right thing. Throw away industry and profit, and there won’t be any thieves

Express yourself completely, then keep quiet.

If you open yourself to insight, you are at one with insight and you can use it completely.

He who defines himself can’t know who he really is.

If you limit yourself to something, you automatically close the door to being so much more.

Knowing others is intelligence; knowing yourself is true wisdom. Mastering others is strength; mastering yourself is true power.

This is as profound and big as it can be.

The soft overcomes the hard. The slow overcomes the fast. Let your workings remain a mystery. Just show people the results.

If you want to get rid of something, you must first allow it to flourish. If you want to take something, you must first allow it to be given.

The path into the light seems dark, the path forward seems to go back, the direct path seems long, true power seems weak, true purity seems tarnished, true steadfastness seems changeable, true clarity seems obscure, the greatest art seems unsophisticated, the greatest love seems indifferent, the greatest wisdom seems childish

True perfection seems imperfect, yet it is perfectly itself. True fullness seems empty, yet it is fully present. True straightness seems crooked. True wisdom seems foolish. True art seems artless.

There is no greater illusion than fear. Whoever can see through all fear will always be safe.

If you keep your mind from judging and aren’t led by the senses, your heart will find peace.

Seeing into darkness is clarity. Knowing how to yield is strength. Use your own light and return to the source of light.

It’s not always about forcing a tough way out to strength. It’s about using your tao to understand what to be.

Those who know don’t talk. Those who talk don’t know

The more prohibitions you have, the less virtuous people will be. The more weapons you have, the less secure people will be. The more subsidies you have, the less self-reliant people will.

When the will to power is in charge, the higher the ideals, the lower the results. Try to make people happy, and you lay the groundwork for misery.

When they think that they know the answers, people are difficult to guide. When they know that they don’t know, people can find their own way.

All streams flow to the sea because it is lower than they are. Humility gives it its power.

If you want to govern the people, you must place yourself below them. If you want to lead the people, you must learn how to follow them.

Patient with both friends and enemies, you accord with the way things are. Compassionate toward yourself, you reconcile all beings in the world

The best athlete wants his opponent at his best. The best general enters the mind of his enemy. The best businessman serves the communal good. The best leader follows the will of the people

When they lose their sense of awe, people turn to religion. When they no longer trust themselves, they begin to depend upon authority

Trying to control the future is like trying to take the master carpenter’s place. When you handle the master carpenter’s tools, chances are that you’ll cut yourself.

Men are born soft and supple; dead, they are stiff and hard. Plants are born tender and pliant; dead, they are brittle and dry. Thus whoever is stiff and inflexible is a disciple of death. Whoever is soft and yielding is a disciple of life.

True words aren’t eloquent; eloquent words aren’t true. Wise men don’t need to prove their point; men who need to prove their point aren’t wise

We need space in order to see, silence in order to hear, sleep in order to carry on with our wakefulness. If the senses are too cluttered with objects, they lose their acuteness and will eventually decay.

Keeping to the receptive allows the creative to arise. Actually, the creative and the receptive are complementary sides of the same process.

Do you want to improve the world: Ramana Maharshi said, Wanting to reform the world without discovering one’s true self is like trying to cover the world with leather to avoid the pain of walking on stones and thorns. It is much simpler to wear shoes.

True wisdom: When I know myself, I know others. When I master myself, I don’t need to master others.

I have three treasures which I preserve and treasure. The first is compassion, the second is frugality, the third is daring not to be first in the world. Whoever has compassion can be brave. Whoever has frugality can be generous. Whoever dares not to be first in the world can become the leader of the world.

Good fortune can be disaster and vice versa. Who can tell how events will be transformed.

The greatest help is wholeheartedly trusting people to resolve their own problems.

Theme #4 Nature of Tao

The Tao is like a bellows: it is empty yet infinitely capable. The more you use it, the more it produces; the more you talk of it, the less you understand

Giving birth and nourishing, having without possessing, acting with no expectations, leading and not trying to control: this is the supreme virtue.

When the ancient Masters said, “If you want to be given everything, give everything up,” they weren’t using empty phrases. Only in being lived by the Tao can you be truly yourself

You don’t act. Tao acts through you. That’s the fulfillment. When you have nothing to attain to, you have everything already. This is the nature of Tao.

When the Tao is lost, there is goodness. When goodness is lost, there is morality. When morality is lost, there is ritual

Deeply profound.

Our mind is like a glass of clear water. If we put salt into the water, it becomes salt water; sugar, it becomes sugar water; shit, it becomes shit water. But originally the water is clear. No thinking, no mind. No mind, no problem

The Mind : It should be like a river gorge with a swan flying overhead; the river has no desire to retain the swan, yet the swan’s passage is traced by its shadow, without any omission. Another example: A mirror will reflect all things perfectly, whether they are beautiful or ugly; it never refuses to show a thing, nor does it retain the thing after it is gone. The mind should be as open as this

Theme #5 Interrogation

Can you love people and lead them without imposing your will?

Can you step back from your own mind and thus understand all things?

What does it mean that hope is as hollow as fear? Hope and fear are both phantoms that arise from thinking of the self. When we don’t see the self as self, what do we have to fear?

What difference between success and failure? Must you value what others value, avoid what others avoid? How ridiculous!
