The Black Swan

Nassim Nicholas Taleb

  • One single observation can invalidate a general statement derived from millennia of confirmatory sightings of millions of white swans.
  • Black Swan Even
    • Outlier: Outside of regular expectation because past events didn’t forecast it.
    • Extreme Impact: If it was mild, it wouldn’t be that important.
    • Explanation after the event (Retrospective): Human mind finds explanations to make it predictable afterwards.
  • What you know cannot really hurt you.
  • Not understanding the black swan events will lead you to a faulty future prediction. So, we need to focus somehow on what we do not know.
  • One who knows how to strategize differently might easily beat those who study strategies but don’t invent new ones.
  • Though we don’t care about the extremes of personalities, that’s where the major and big problems come from; even when they are rare.
  • One should avoid using terms made of knowledge to describe not-knowing.
  • There can be no knowing except that your map is not the territory. If you over-analyze, you again fall into the same pit.
  • Knowing the grayness of ideas, theories, philosophies can be a robust way to tackle black swan events.

What we call here a Black Swan (and capitalize it) is an event with the following three attributes. First, it is an outlier, as it lies outside the realm of regular expectations, because nothing in the past can convincingly point to its possibility. Second, it carries an extreme impact (unlike the bird). Third, in spite of its outlier status, human nature makes us concoct explanations for its occurrence after the fact, making it explainable and predictable

I stop and summarize the triplet: rarity, extreme impact, and retrospective (though not prospective) predictability
