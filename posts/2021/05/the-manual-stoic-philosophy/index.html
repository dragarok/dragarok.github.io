The Manual- Stoic Philosophy

  • If you have a favorite cup, remember that it is only a cup that you prefer-if it is broken, you can bear it.
  • In preparing for any action, remind yourself of the nature of the action.
  • When you are feeling upset, angry, or sad, don’t blame another for your state of mind. Your condition is the result of your own opinions and interpretations.
  • Don’t take satisfaction in possessions and achievements that are not your own. If a horse were to say, “I am handsome,” his pride may be excusable. But if you boast, " I have a handsome horse," you are claiming merit that is not yours.
  • In a voyage when ship is anchored and you go ashore for supplies, you may amuse yourself with collecting some seashells and pretty stones but your thought is still alert for captain’s call. You may need to drop your treasures and run back to the boat at any time. Likewise is life, you need to remain steadfast in pursuing your mission, always willing to shed distractions.
  • Don’t wish that all things will go well with you, but that you will go well with all things.
  • The only thing that can impede your will is your will itself.
  • If you meet temptation, use self-control; if you meet pain, use fortitude; if you meet revulsion, use patience.
  • Don’t say of anything " I have lost it, " but rather “I have given it back.”
  • For as long as the SOURCE entrusts something to your hands, treat it as something borrowed, like a traveler at an inn.
  • Stop making excuses like , “If I don’t cling tightly to my money and possessions, I’ll end up on the street. "
  • Along this path of philosophy:
    • Don’t strive to be celebrated for anything.
    • If you are praised by yourself, be skeptical for yourself. For it is no easy feat to hold onto your inner harmony while collecting accolades. While grasping for one, you are likely to drop for other.
  • Sympathize with a grieving person. Don’t just push your thoughts to others. Cry with them. Your tears will be outward, not inward.
  • When it comes across a “bad omen”, distinguish between reality and interpretation. Remind yourself that everything points to good luck if I interpret it that way. Whatever life brings, I can use it to my advantage.
  • Continually remind yourself that you are a mortal being, and someday you will die. This will inspire you to not waste your time on fruitless activities, like stewing over grievances and striving after possessions.
  • If you find yourself acting to impress others, or avoiding action out of fear about what they might think, you have left the path. Find satisfaction in following your philosophy. If you want to be respected, start by respecting yourself.
  • Find your significance within yourself. Within your own sphere of power-that is where you have the greatest consequence. Can you really give someone happiness and satisfaction-things that are in their own spheres of power and not yours? And can you give someone something that you, yourself, don’t even have?
  • If you can acquire riches without losing your honor and self-respect, then do it. But if you lose what is dearest to you, no amount of money can make up for it.
  • Do you envy someone who is popular at work, invited to parties, and spoken of in social circles? If they attract love because of positive character qualities, be glad for them. If they attract attention because of negative character qualities, be glad that you don’t have them.
  • Don’t expect to equal anyone in effect without putting forth a similar effort. A person who rarely leaves home, who doesn’t converse with, praise, and encourage others, will not attract friends.
  • Do you have anything good in place of the invitation? Yes-you have the pleasure of not making small talk with people you don’t really like, not praising someone you don’t admire, and not mingling with lackeys.
  • You ought to react in the same way you did when it was your neighbor’s property that was damaged.
  • If someone tried to take control of your body and make you a slave, you would fight for freedom. Yet how easily you hand over your mind to anyone who insults you. When you dwell on their words and let them dominate your thoughts, you make them your master.

Vocation

  • Fancying yourself to be a gladiator, orator or a philosopher but never beginning training in earnest means you are a dabbler who samples many fields, but when the time comes to commit to one, you move on to the next.
  • To find your vocation, first consider your talents and inclinations. Do you have the back, shoulders, and thighs of a wrestler? Or the potential and determination to gain them through exercise?
  • Whatever your vocation, pursue it wholeheartedly. Consider, choose, and commit.

Duties

  • Duties are determined by relations. If a man is your father, relationship implies the duties of listening to his counsel, following his instructions, patiently receiving his correction, and helping to take care of him in his old age.
  • Fulfill your duties to the best of your ability.
  • Before judging anyone’s behavior look to your own. If he insults or rejects you, don’t stoop to his level and retaliate in anger.

Circumstances

  • If you suppose events to be good or evil in themselves, when life doesn’t go as you wish you will inevitably blame the author.
  • When you understand that the outside events don’t touch your deepest self- what matters is your interpretation and reaction - you can use any circumstance to your benefit.

Behaving in public

  • Be the same person in public as in private.
  • Avoid idle chatter. Refuse to participate in gossip- tearing down, inflating, and judging other people.
  • Among friends, shift the conversation to worthy topics; among strangers, stay silent.
  • Avoid taking oaths as much as possible.
  • If a friend tells you that someone has criticized or insulted you, say, “They must not know about my other faults, or they would have pointed out those, too”.
  • Avoid extremes of elation at a win and devastation at a loss.
  • Keep your language free of obscenities. Don’t dip into the gutter in search of cheap laughs.
  • If you are unsure of how to act in a given social situation, ask yourself how Socrates or Zeno would behave. Model yourself after the wisest.
  • If you win the adoration of others by pretending to be someone you’re not, you may gain high status- but you will lose out on the fulfillment of a life best suited to your attributes and qualities.

Desires

  • Consider the cost of pursuing potential pleasure, every consequence that may follow from acquiring or experiencing it, and how you will feel about it the next day.
  • Consider how you will feel about yourself tomorrow if you resist the temptation today.
  • Once you let your appetite exceed what is necessary and useful, desire knows no bounds.

Thinking

  • Watch for obstacles and errors in your line of thought.
  • Tread carefully, taking care not to stumble to illogic and unreason.
  • Whenever someone helps or hinders you, or praises or criticizes you, remember that they see you only through the lens of their own impressions. If they act or speak from a warped perspective, they hurt themselves- not you.
  • For if someone mistakes truth for falsehood, the truth is not harmed, but only the person deceived. Keep this in mind, gently turn away any insult or injury. “It seems right to them, though they are mistaken”.

Body and Mind

  • Care for your body as needed, but put your main energies and efforts into cultivating your mind.

Tit for tat

  • Everything has two handles: one by which it can be carried, and one by which it cannot. If a friend treats you unfairly, don’t try to grasp the situation by the handle of retribution- it will burn your hand.

Illogical Conclusions

  • “I am richer than you, so I am more valuable than you.”
  • “I am more eloquent than you, therefore I am superior to you.”

Logical Conclusions

  • Examples:
    • “I am richer than you, so I can buy more things than you.”
    • “I am more eloquent than you, therefore my speech is more polished than yours.”
    • Does a person bathe quickly? Don’t say that they bathe poorly, but only quickly.
    • Does a person drink a lot of wine? Don’t say that they drink badly, but only in abundance.
  • Unless you perfectly understand other’s motives, how can you judge them? Don’t mistake your impressions for the whole truth.

Reacting to others

  • If anyone calls you ignorant and says you know nothing, be sure that you are now a true student of philosophy.
  • Sheep don’t spit out grass to show the farmer how much they’ve eaten- they ruminate on it, digest it, and then display the results in wool and milk.
  • Once you have reigned in your bodily appetites, don’t brag about it. Ask for water, but don’t announce at the table, “I only drink water.”
  • Don’t make a spectacle of self-deprivation. When you fast, tell no one.

Philosophy

  • First topic of philosophy is practical application of principles.
  • Second topic is understanding the reasons behind the principles.
  • Third topic is verifying the principles through logic.
  • Philosophy is for living, not just learning.
