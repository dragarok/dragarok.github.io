Getting Things Done

Allen, David

GTD

GTD Intro

  • Anything that causes you to overreact or underact can control you, and often does.
  • Anything that doesn’t belong where it is, the way it is, is an “open loop”, which will be pulling on your attention if it’s not appropriately managed.
  • First of all, if it’s on your mind, your mind isn’t clear. Anything you consider unfinished must be captured in a trusted system outside your mind.
  • Research that significant part of your psyche cannot help but keep track of your open loops, and not as an intelligent, positive motivator, but as a detractor from anything else you need or want to think about.
  • As soon as you attach a “should”, “need to”, or “ought to” to an item, it becomes an incomplete.
  • Project list is not meant to hold plans or details about your projects themselves, nor should you try to keep it arranged by priority or size or urgency.
  • The calendar should show only the “hard landscape” around which you do the rest of your actions.
  • You will often have ideas that you will want to keep about projects but that are not necessarily next actions. Those ideas fall into the broad category of “PROJECT SUPPORT MATERIALS”.
  • One of the biggest problems with PMS is that they blend a few actionable things with a large amount of data and material that has value but no action attached.
  • When the non-actionable items aren’t properly managed, they clog up the whole process.
  • MAGIC KEY TO SUSTAINABILITY OF THE PROCESS IS THE WEEKLY REVIEW.
  • Project types:
    • Those that still have your attention even after you’ve determined their next actions.
    • Those about which potentially useful ideas and supportive details just show up ad hoc.
  • Sense of anxiety and guilt doesn’t come from having too much to do; it’s the automatic result of breaking agreements with yourself.
  • Use your mind to think about things, rather than think of them.

Defining anything in GTD

  1. Describe, in a single written sentence, your intended successful outcome for this problem or situation. [ INTENDED OUTCOME ]
  2. Write down the very next physical action required to move the situation forward. [ NEXT ACTION ]

Calendar

  1. Time Specific Actions.
  2. Day Specific Actions.
  3. Day Specific Information.

Tools

  1. Someday/Maybe Lists
  2. Tickler System

Reviewing Process

  1. All your PROJECTS, ACTIVE PROJECT PLANS, and NEXT ACTIONS, AGENDAS, WAITING FOR, and even SOMEDAY/MAYBE lists should be reviewed once a week.

Leveraging contexts

  1. Asking why a. Defines success. b. Creates decision-making criteria. c. Aligns resources. d. Motivates. e. Clarifies focus. f. Expands options.
  2. Capturing Tasks a. Full capturing. b. Clarifying and deciding actions for all the input you’ll want to externalize and capture.
  3. Incompletion Triggers a. Process the top item first. b. Process one item at a time. c. Never put anything back to “INBOX”.
  4. Record dates a. On everything that you hand off to others.

Hidden Projects

  1. Current activities.
  2. Higher-horizon interests.
  3. Current Problems, issues and opportunities.

Frequent Mistakes

  1. Not setting inbox to zero. ==> Mind doesn’t trust the system unless you take everything into the system. No papers, no anything.
  2. Not clarifying the next action. ==> Next task on filing taxes might be to call or email or discuss.
  3. Project List is not current. ==> People resist because it forces you to get clear on your larger commitments.
  4. Someday/Maybe List ==> Out of sight is out of mind. An active someday/maybe list gives us freedom to park commitments for later. Use this properly. Without one, they’ll linger in your mind or get stale on the projects list.
  5. Not taking out time to plan ==> If you don’t take time to plan, you’ll not be ready for the surprises.
  6. Putting everything on the calendar ==> Calendar is only meant for data and time specific actions/information.
  7. Taking too long to do the weekly review ==> Something’s wrong if it is taking > 1.5 hrs. Don’t use this time to clear the backlog.
  8. Not look at your lists ==> Looking at it regularly, mind learns to trust your GTD to track open loops.
  9. Investing in tools and not habits ==> Tool is not as important as building the right habits. a. Capture what has your attention. b. Clarify what it means. c. Organize where it belongs. d. Reflect regularly. e. Engage make a trusted choice.
  10. Addiction to Stress ==> Being present with what you are doing.

More nuances to the process

  • Inbox should be processed based on actionability of the item.
  • No task should be inside inbox for too long. It either needs to go to References list or needs to go to Actionable List.
  • When you put items into inbox, they can be more abstract such as “Plan to do something” but when they are going into Actionable list, the task needs to be visible and actionable.
  • Once it’s an actionable task, you decide whether it takes more than 2 minutes to do it. Overhead of tracking >2 min task is significant.
  • Try delegating the task before you start doing it.
  • Now, if the task is too big, it will go into a projects list.
  • The tasks that needs to be done immediately will go into NEXT ACTIONS list.
  • If there is a task where you are waiting for someone after delegation, it will be in WAITING FOR list. You will have a scheduled date where you check the status of that work with the person.

Projects

  • Deliverables/Intended Outcomes
  • Objectives
  • Descriptions
  • At least one actionable item so that it’s a WIP not stale meat.

Contexts

  • Tags to be put on next actions list.
  • Where action is to be done, what equipment you need to do it.
  • How many contexts and how many next actions depends on how your day is structured.
  • Agenda Contexts for Persons if there are meetings.

Calendar

  • Only have things that are time and date sensitive into the calendar.

Weekly Review

  • Make sure each project has at least one NEXT ACTION.
  • Make sure NEXT ACTION tasks are thos eyou really want to or need to do if you have time during the next week. Else, move it to SOMEDAY/MAYBE list.
  • Check SOMEDAY/MAYBE list to get anything back to action.

Trigger Lists

  • Simple keywords to navigate the week.

Read/Review Folder

  • Tasks that are not actual tasks but you could do it when you have some spare time to kill.

Tickler File

  • Physical Goods needed on a particular day such as tickets.
