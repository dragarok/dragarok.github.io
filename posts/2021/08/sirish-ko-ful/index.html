Book summaryAug 26, 2021 3 min read

Sirish ko Ful

The way this novel takes an approach on the story and the narrative of Love, rape, life, being, and flowers is as good as it could be. The narrative takes us on a journey of the mental state of Suyog, wants us to sympathize with it somehow, and to understand how people get away with their horrendous acts.

Suyog, Shivaraj, Alcoholism, Identity Crisis, and the unspoken words all over the place. Two different female figures: One resembling beauty, and another one resembling rebelliousness. But, the irony of the story is that rebel stops fighting and gives up. The book itself talks of ironies. Views people have compared to acts people are shown to be totally different. This is the irony of life. It’s so easy to talk about a philosophy, but difficult to live one.

The story takes on the identity of different women who he raped, belied, and used for sexual pleasure. The story takes that idea of macho, and destroys it with his feelings for Bari. Who is not fragile in this world after all? People break but under specific circumstances. The talk of Suyog with his friend during war about tears resembles a strong dissonance to his personality about his nature towards feelings of love.

The symbolism of the flowers, road, carnivorous plant, and stone as god are all so deeply philosophical. She has used all these elements frequently in subtle way through the story to give a bigger narrative than words– to show irony like none other.

The self-doubt, torture, and alcoholism trying to define love is a strange one here. It seems at points that Bari is not doing justice to him as well but getting to the end, we understand the true nature of Suyog– alcoholic, fed up, and living in the face of the number of horrors he admits he has done.

Even with the freedom Bari has in the story, it still ends up in death for her. This is a very depressing narrative in the sense that even Bari could not circumvent the grasp of the impious act. This is a very bleak reality that most girls face, but are afraid to even express. The level of pain with which they live is so heart-wrenching that there are no words to describe what they feel. Let’s not even glorify that they accept it. Let’s not write words to sympathize. Everything I will write will make it worse. I just want to take a moment and realize this truth fully. I hope I will be there for someone to express their repressions with ease some day. It’s very hard for me to write anything more than that.

This is an excellent take on psychology of a war veteran, a macho, a rapist, a killer, a lover, an alcoholic, and a sadistic vagabond. The conflict, the philosophical takes on aspects of life, the struggle to express feelings, the sexual deluge, and the idea of flowers compared to individual is indeed brilliant.
