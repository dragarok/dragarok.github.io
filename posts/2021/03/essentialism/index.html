Essentialism

Summary

  • Our generation is unprepared of the wide availability of choices and has become overwhelmed with it. “Decision Fatigue” is widespread now.
  • Connectedness <–> Social Pressure
  • Opinion Overload but not just Information Overload
  • Priority word means the very first thing but over the last 150 years, we pluralized the word.
  • Don’t force execution: instead create system to remove obstacles to make execution smooth and easy.
  • By trying to operate with incompatible strategies, you undermine your ability to be competitive as done by Airlines Companies mentioned in the story.
  • Saying YES now, you miss out on something meaningful later if you say YES just out of the fear of missing out.
  • Latin word for decision is “cis/cid” that forms the base of the word decision means “to cut or to kill”
  • In modern times, the work boundary has encroached the family territory and it’s yours to manage it.
  • Boundaries if you break them once are like sandcastle. You will be expected to break them again and again. So, stick to them.

Endowment Effect

Tendency to undervalue things that aren’t ours and overvalue things because we already own them. Sunk Cost Bias: More money you invest, more you want to invest even if it’s losing you money.

  • How to not be trapped in it?

    See ref:Endowment-Effect for what you need to change.

  • What you should know

    1. Admit failure to begin success
    2. Stop trying to force a fit
    3. Get a neutral second opinion

Status Quo Bias

Tendency to continue doing something simply because you have always done this way. People tend to use the expense limits set from previous year due to which they don’t have a lot of improvement over their expenses. Instead, a new concept can be used. “ZERO-BASED BUDGETING”: You don’t start the expense by subtracting from previous year’s but start a new from zero and keep adding to it. It detects exaggerated budget requests, finds out obsolete operations and be clearer in purpose in terms of expense involved.

Why Essentialism?

  • To choose how we spend our time and energy
  • Finding valuable things from noise
  • Trade-offs are real and we need to value them.

Essentialists explore more options than non-essentialists.

Questions you need to answer first

These questions are at the heart of essentialism. Ask yourself these questions first.

Saying YES or NO

We typically say YES to things to please people. But our highest contribution might be saying NO.

Lack of Clarity

  • Sort of Clear vs Really Clear is a difference that people don’t note much but matters a LOT.
  • Lack of clarity means people experience confusion, stress and frustration.
  • Ask “How will we know when we’re done?'”

  • What happens when teams lack clarity?

    1. Playing Politics: Trying to win the manager. Creating and focusing on social game’s goal rather than the actual task’s goal.
    2. Leaderless Teams: People start acting on their own short-term activities which in no way contribute to the overall goal they are working to achieve.

  • Example of a clear Vision

    • “To build 150 affordable, green, storm-resistant homes for families living in the Lower 9th Ward.” is example of mission statement that clarifies what the end outcome should be so that each task can be discriminated based on if it closes the gap between the goal and the current situation rather than saying “To help people after the disaster.”

Say NO without ruining things

  • Separate the decision from the relationship

    You make sure the NO is about the task at hand rather than about your relationship with the person.

  • Saying NO doesn’t mean you have to use word NO

    • I am flattered that you thought of me but I am currently occupied.
    • I would very much like to but I’m over-committed.

  • Focus more on the trade-off

    More you think about what you’re giving up when you say YES to someone, easier it is to say NO.

  • Remind yourself that everyone is selling something

    People get disappointed with you in the short-term but start respecting you for your honesty. Generally, people meet all YES people around and thus you stand different and tall in terms of respect.

  • Tips when saying NO

    1. Awkward Pause: 4 5 seconds.
    2. Soft No: “No but”
    3. Let me check my calendar and get back to you.
    4. Email Bouncebacks
    5. Say “Yes. What should I deprioritize?” Useful in office situations.
    6. Say it with humor.
    7. You are welcome to X. I am willing to Y. : Indirect way of saying I can’t do this specific thing for you.

The Law of Vital Few

  • Japan used to be like China back then with low-cost:low-quality goods.
  • Joseph Mures Buran did a quality control experiment in Japan.
  • By resolving tiny fraction of problems and improving things that were truly important, “Made in Japan” has a different meaning now.
  • This is also known as the famous “Pareto Principle” or the “80 / 20 rule”.

Warren Buffett’s Investments

  • Rather than make hundreds of right investment decisions, invest in only few that he was absolutely sure of and bet heavily on them.
  • Buffet owes his 90% of wealth to just 10 investments.

Changing Perspective

Go from “What do I have to give up?” ——>“What do I want to go big on?”

Go from “What will I say yes to?” ——-> “What will I say no to?”

“To attain knowledge, add things every day” ——> “To attain wisdom, subtract things every day.” -Lao Tzu

Reducing friction with other person: “What obstacles or bottlenecks are holding you back from achieving X, and how can I help remove these?”

Go from “How much do I value this item?” ——-> “If I did not own this item, how much would I pay to obtain it?”

Go from “How will I feel if I miss out on this opportunity?” ——> “If I did not have this opportunity, how much would I be willing to sacrifice in order to obtain it?”

Go from “I should say YES to this project because of following things.” —–> “If I wasn’t already involved in this project, how hard would I work to get on it?”

Explore

What must you build on and for your journey?

  • Set aside time to look and listen
  • Space to think
  • Permission to play
  • Wisdom to Sleep
  • Discipline to apply highly selective criteria to choices you make.

  • Examples

    • Newton’s uninterrupted space during the plague allowed him to explore the universe.
    • Bill Gates goes to read books for a week twice a year. No cellphones, No connection.

Being Playful

Being busy and being hard on yourself means you don’t understand where creativity flows in. You get insights when you truly enjoy something out of the task you were pondering about. You let go of the worries and actually find yourself thinking out of the box.

  • Example

    • Einstein credited more for his ability to imagine and fantasize more than for absorbing knowledge.

Hell Yeah or No

  • Examples

    • Derek Sivers : If you feel a total and utter conviction to do something, say HELL YEAH. Else it’s a NO.
    • Company that employs candidate based on his one day work in an office.

Eliminate

How to Eliminate?

  • Write down the opportunity.
  • Write down 3 Minimum Criteria to pass in order to be considered
  • Write down 3 Extreme/ Ideal Criteria to pass in order to be considered

  • Evaluation

    • If it doesn’t pass all 3 of the minimum criteria, drop it.
    • If it doesn’t pass any 2 of the extreme criteria, drop it this time too.

Ways to not commit instantly

  1. Stop making casual commitments
  2. Pause before you speak
  3. Get over the fear of missing out: Use Reverse Pilot

  • Reverse Piloting

    To test an assumption that something is invaluable, rather than asking if people need it: you remove it completely. If people notice it, it’s valuable. Otherwise it’s not.

Kinds of Boundaries

  • Why do you need boundaries?

    In the working world, people try to use our sprinklers to water their own grass all the time. This may come in the form of a boss who puts you on a committee for her pet project, a colleague who asks for your input on a report or presentation or proposal she hasn’t taken the time to perfect yet herself, or a colleague who stops you in the hallway and talks your ear off when you have an important meeting to get to or a vital phone call to make or critical work on your desk

  • Dealing with boundary issues

    • Don’t rob people of their problems

      • When people make their problem our problem, we aren’t helping them; we’re enabling them. Once we take their problem for them, all we’re doing is taking away their ability to solve it.

    • Find your dealbreakers

      You need to find yours first for yourself and others: it may seem unrealistic if you don’t know yourself. How will you expect other people to respect them or even figure them out?

    • Craft Social Contracts

      Even if there are personal differences between you and people you work with, have an understanding up front about how not to waste each other’s time and do what’s essential in a way that the task at hand demands.

Execute

Expecting the Unexpected using Buffers

  • Essentialist looks ahead: creates plans, contingencies, buffer for the unforseen.
  • Use good time to create buffer for the bad.

  • Example

    • Norway enormously benefited from windfall taxes from oil but invested much of it’s good fortune in endowment having a government fund of over $400 billions.
    • Amundsen when going to the South Pole had all contingencies ready. For e.g. Instead of having one tonnes of food, he had taken three and so on in many different aspects of his travel thus being successful in it.

  • Methods

    • Conduct Scenario Planning

      • What risks do you face on this project?
      • What is the worst-case scenario?
      • What would the social effects of this be?
      • What would the financial impact of this be?
      • How can you invest to reduce risks or strengthen financial or social resilience?

Constraints

  • Check over the constraints more than anything else.

  • Example

    • If you have slow walking person in a group, you make them walk on the front so that the whole group can walk as a group. Or more you can carry backpack among others to speed up the slowest one. So, instead of trying to improve every aspect you try to improve only that’s the most problematic.

Motivation

  • The most effective forms of human motivation is “PROGRESS”.
  • Focus on minimum viable progress.
  • Do the minimum viable preparation.
  • Visually reward Progress

  • Example

    • Michael Phelps would be disciplined and visualize the whole competition end to end. He would do all the steps of the imagination as to feel that the final swimming is also a part of that one and did it as he dreamed of it.

Routines

  • Habit = Cue + Routine + Reward
  • Cue: Trigger that tells you to go to auto mode and choose habit to use.
  • Routine: Behavior itself
  • Reward: Figure out if this particular habit is worth remembering for the future.
  • Once the loop gets done multiple times, it strengthens itself.
  • So, rather than changing the habit : we need to find the cue that is triggering non essential activity and associate that same cue with something else.

  • Example

    • Every time you see McDonalds, you want to get a burger. Now, every time you see it, you just go and buy some fresh vegetables or eat some salad by the side. You change the cue to some new habit.

  • Mixing Up Routines

    • Jack Dorsey has his days labelled for different things such as Innovation, Business Meetings, Marketing and Growth and so on.

  • Tackle Your Routines One By One

    Often we feel the urge to change everything this very instant and slowly when we don’t do it as per expectations, we totally leave the process. So, go slow and go long.

Mindfulness

  • Kairos : Greek god for time that says opportune, right and different. It’s qualitative and experienced only in the moment itself.
  • Multitasking itself is not the enemy of Essentialism; pretending we can “multifocus” is.

  • How to be mindful?

    • Figure out what’s important right now
    • Get the future out of your head. Just note down what you want to achieve as a result of today so that you know it’s somewhere down and you don’t need to worry.

  • Pause that refreshes

    • Closing your eye after you come from work just before entering your home and taking a long breath can give you so much that you take a pause to not bring work pressures into home.

What being essentialist looks like?

“Once you become an Essentialist, you will find that you aren’t like everybody else. When other people are saying yes, you will find yourself saying no. When other people are doing, you will find yourself thinking. When other people are speaking, you will find yourself listening. When other people are in the spotlight, vying for attention, you will find yourself waiting on the sidelines until it is time to shine. While other people are padding their résumés and building out their LinkedIn profiles, you will be building a career of meaning. While other people are complaining (read: bragging) about how busy they are, you will just be smiling sympathetically, unable to relate. While other people are living a life of stress and chaos, you will be living a life of impact and fulfillment.”

Some essentialist tasks

  • Choosing not to watch T.V. when traveling for business.
