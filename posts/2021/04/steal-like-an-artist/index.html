Steal Like an Artist

  • By copying from great artists, you start learning in a way thats real good.

Don’t look at the world looking at what’s “good” and what’s “bad”. There’s only stuff worth stealing, and stuff that’s not worth stealing.

Everything that needs to be said has already been said. But, since no one was listening, everything must be said again. – Andre Gide

“What is originality? Undetected plagiarism. " – William Ralph Inge

Being a collector

  • Hoarders collect indiscriminately, artists collect selectively.
  • At a time, chew one thinker – writer, artist, activist, role model – you really love. Study everything there is to know about that thinker.
  • It’s not the book you start with, it’s the book that book leads you to. Collect books, even if you don’t plan on reading them right away.
  • See something worth stealing? Put it in the swipe file. Need a little inspiration? Open up the swipe file.

If I’d waited to know who I was or what I was about before I started “being creative,” well, I’d still be sitting around trying to figure myself out instead of making things.

Fake it till you make it.

  1. Pretend to be something you are not until you are – fake it until you’re successful, until everybody sees you the way you want them to.
  2. Pretend to be making something until you actually make something.

Start copying what you love. Copy copy copy copy. At the end of the copy you will find your self. – Yohji Yamamoto

Copying

  • Plagiarism is different from copying. Plagiarism is trying to pass someone else’s work as your own. Copying is about reverse-engineering.
  • Steps:
    • First, you have to figure who to copy.
    • Second, you have to figure out what to copy.
  • Who to copy is easier : your heroes, people you love, people you’re inspired by, the people you want to be.
  • What you copy is trickier: Don’t just steal their style, steal the thinking behind their style. You don’t want to look like your heroes, you want to see like your heroes.
  • The reason to copy your heroes and their style is so that you might somehow get a glimpse of their minds.

Those who don’t want to imitate anything, produce nothing. – Salvador Dali

If you copy from one author, it’s plagiarism but if you copy from many, it’s research. – Wilson Mizner

Imitation to Emulation

  • Now after you are done imitating, you have to move to emulating them. Emulation goes one step further from copying , breaking through into your own thing.
  • You have to fit their styles to your own to potentially use your strengths and weaknesses.
  • Many comedians try to emulate their heroes, fall short, and end up doing their own work.
    • e.g. David Letterman tried to copy Jhonny Carson but ended up David Letterman and Conan O’ Brien tried to be David but ended up being himself.

It is our failure to become our perceived ideal that ultimately defines us and makes us unique. – Conan O’ Brien

  • What you fall short on when copying your heroes. What’s in there that makes you different? Amplify that and transform it into your own work.

What to write?

  • Write what you know is the generic answer everyone gives when asked for what to write.
  • Best advice is to write what you like. Write the kind of story you like best- something you would want to read.
  • When we love a piece of work, we’re desperate for more. We crave sequels. Why not channel that desire into something productive?
  • Think about your favorite work and your creative heroes.
    • What did they miss?
    • What didn’t they make?
    • What could have been done better?
    • If they were alive today, what would they be making today?
    • If all your favorite creators got together and collaborated, what would they make with you leading the crew?

Separation of Spaces

  • Digital and Analog Space.
  • When you get to the analog space in your room, you pretend it’s craft time.
  • Scribble on paper, cut it up, and tape the pieces together.
  • Stand up while you’re working.
  • Pin things on the wall and look for patterns.
  • Spread things around your space and sort through them.

Side Projects

  • It’s the side projects that really take off.
  • Stuff that you thought was just messing around. Stuff that’s play.

Be bored; Be creative

  • He gets his ideas when he’s bored. That’s why he irons his shirts, washes his dishes, stare on the wall or a really long walk when he’s running out of ideas.

Avoiding work is the way to focus the mind. – Maira Kalman

Diverse ideas

  • If you love different things, you just keep spending time with them.
  • By letting diverse ideas talk to each other, something new will pop up.

Hobbies

  • A hobby is something creative that’s just for you.
  • You don’t try to make money or get famous off it, you just do it because it makes you happy.
  • Something that gives but doesn’t take.

Being famous

Enjoy your obscurity while it lasts. Use it.

  • The main thing is Do good work and share it with people.
  • Steps:
    1. Wonder at something.
    2. Invite others to wonder with you. You should wonder at things nobody else is wandering about.
    3. Give your secrets away and in doing so, they’ll reward you by buying the things you are selling.

Using the internet

  • Internet just doesn’t need to be a place for finished and polished ideas; it can be incubator for ideas that aren’t fully formed, a birthing center.
  • You don’t have to share everything- sometimes it’s much better if you don’t. Show just a little bit of what you’re working on. Sketch, doodle, or a snippet.
  • Share a handy tip you’ve discovered while working. Or a link to an interesting article. Mention a good book you’re reading.

It isn’t necessary that you leave home. Sit at your desk and listen. Don’t even listen, just wait. Don’t wait, be still and alone. The whole world will offer itself to you. – Franz Kafka

People around you

  • If you ever find you’re the most talented person in the room, then you are in the wrong room.
  • If you truly love somebody’s work, you shouldn’t need a response from them.
  • Find the most talented person in the room, and if it’s not you, go stand next to him. Hang out with him. Try to be helpful.

Good work

  • Good work often appears to be effortless.
  • People will say, “Why didn’t I think of that? "

Praise file

  • Instead of keeping a rejection file, keep a praise file for yourself. Use it sparingly - don’t get lost in the past glory - but keep it around for when you need the lift.

Day Job

  • A day job puts you in path of other human beings. Learn from them, steal from them.
  • The worst thing a day job does is take time away from you, but it makes up for that by giving you a daily routine in which you can schedule a regular time for your creative pursuits.
  • The trick is to find day job that pays decently, doesn’t make you want to vomit, and leaves you with enough energy to make things in your spare time.

Seinfield’s Box

  • Instead of getting work done, your goal is to just fill a box.
  • After a few days, you wil have a chain.
  • Just keep at it and the chain will grow longer every day. You’ll like seeing that chain, especially when you get a few weeks under your belt.
  • Then, only job is to not break the chain.

Random

Nothing is more paralyzing than the idea of limitless possibilities. The idea that you can do anything is absolutely terrifying.

  • It’s often what an artist chooses to leave out that makes the art interesting. What isn’t shown versus what is.

Starting it up

  • Start your swipe file.
  • Go to the library.
  • Buy a notebook and use it.
  • Get yourself a calendar.
  • Start your logbook.
  • Start a blog.
  • Take a nap.
