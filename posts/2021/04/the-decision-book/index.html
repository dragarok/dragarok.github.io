The Decision Book

EisenHower Task Management

  • Ask yourself: When will I deal with the things that are important, but not urgent? When will I take the time to deal with important tasks before they become urgent? This is the field for strategic, long-term decisions.

Assessing the value of Investments in company’s portfolio

  • Cash cows: High market share but low growth rate. Doesn’t cost much but promise high returns.
  • Stars : High market share and high growth rate. But growth rate devours money. Hope is that they will turn into cash cows. So, invest.
  • Problem Children: Have high growth potential but a low share of the market. With a lot of financial support, can be turned into stars. So, tough decision.
  • Dogs: Business units with a low share in a saturated market. Held only if they have a value other than a financial one ( e.g. vanity project or for a friend ).

Classifying projects with project portfolio matrix

  • Cost not just in terms of money but also in terms of resources such as friends involved, energy and psychological stress.

Interpreting the results

  • Reject projects if there is nothing you can learn from them and if they do not correspond to your overriding vision.
  • Projects that don’t correspond to your vision are interesting but will not help you achieve your objective. Try to change the project so that it serves your vision.
  • If a project corresponds to your vision, but you are learning nothing new, look for somebody else to do it for you.
  • If you are learning something and achieving your vision, that’s the jackpot!

Finding your greatest Strength

People think they know what they’re good at, but they’re usually wrong. – Peter F. Drucker

  • Whenever you have an important decision to make, write down what you expect to happen. After a year, compare your expectation with the actual outcome.
  • Over the years, you will compare your expectations with actual results, give yourself feedback and recognize where and what type of improvement is needed.

Setting goals : Too high or Too low

The greatest danger for most of us is not that our aim is too high and we miss it, but that it is too low and we reach it. – Michelangelo

Is having more choices good for us?

  • Intuitively we generally think more means better.
  • We believe the more options we take into consideration, the better our final decision. i.e. The more choices we have, the happier we are.
  • But sometimes the opposite is true: the greater the choice, the higher our expectations - the more we worry that we will make the wrong decision.
  • Paradox of Choice by Sheena Iyengar
  • So, In restaurants, pick the first dish on the menu that you like the look of, then immediately close the menu. Because the more options you juggle in your mind, the more dissatisfied you will be.

Building something new

  • For development of a new car:
    • List out all the relevant parameters: e.g. Vehicle type, Target Group
    • List as many attributes as possible to each parameter.
  • SCAMPER Checklist by Bob Eberle helps to reconfigure an existing idea or product.
  • 7 key questions developed by Alex Osborn:
    • Substitute - People , component , materials.
    • Combine - Combine with other functions or things.
    • Adapt - Adapt functions or visual appearance.
    • Modify - Modify the size, shape, texture or acoustics.
    • Put to other use - Other, new combined uses.
    • Eliminate - Reduce, simplify, eliminate something superfluous.
    • Reverse - Use conversely, invert.

Personal Awareness

  • Divides into four different types:
    1. A: Describes characteristics and experiences that we are aware of ourselves and that we like to tell others about.
    2. B: “Hidden” Describes things that we know about ourselves but choose not reveal to others.
    3. C: Things that we don’t know about ourselves that other see clearly. Feedback can be enlightening. There are things that we think we are expressing clearly, but which others interpret completely differently.
    4. D: Something unknown arises to surface from our consciousness that neither we nor others are aware of. e.g. In a dream.

Cognitive Dissonance

  • State of mind when our actions are not consistent with our beliefs.

Finding out what you can forget and what you will take to the future

  • You define a time-frame. e.g. Last year, Marriage, Founding of a company to today.
  • Think back to the start of the period, either alone or in a group.
  • Add following to the timeline:
    • Goals ( at the time )
    • What you learned
    • Obstacles you overcame
    • Successes
    • People Involved

Personal Potential Trap

  • Three Curves.
    • My expectations of myself.
    • Other’s expectations of me.
    • My actual Achievements.
  • The trap opens as soon as other’s expectations of you and your actual achievements diverge too much.
  • So, the way to go is to promise 80 and deliver 120.

Nobody googles counterarguments

  • We interpret information in such a way that it affirms our existing assumption or conviction.
  • We block out information that contradicts our opinions.

Taking one time incident to generalization

  • You have a car accident with a Volkswagen. – VW makes awful cars.
  • You once had a pretty girlfriend from Poland. – All Polish people are good.
